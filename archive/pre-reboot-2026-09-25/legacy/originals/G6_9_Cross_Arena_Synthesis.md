## 2. Universal game-graph core

### 2.1 Strict attractor

For a finite turn-based graph and exact target `T`, `Attr(T)` is the least fixed point containing `T` and closed under attacker-existential and defender-universal predecessors. This operation is topology-independent once the game graph, target and shortcut semantics are fixed.

### 2.2 Branch-Kernel Factorization — two targets

Let `A1=Attr(T1)`, `A2=Attr(T2)`, `B=A1∪A2`, and `P=Attr(T1∪T2)\B`. Define the **branch kernel** `K` as residual defender states whose replies all enter `B`, while the reply set is not wholly contained in `A1` or wholly contained in `A2`.

**Theorem.** `P` is exactly the residual backward closure generated by `K`. Therefore a strict pure bridge exists **if and only if** the branch kernel is nonempty.

**Proof sketch.** If `P` is nonempty, take a minimum union-attractor-rank state in `P`. It cannot be attacker-owned: an attacker move into a constituent attractor would put the state in that constituent, while a lower-rank pure successor contradicts minimality. Hence it is defender-owned. All successors are lower-rank union-attractor states, and minimality places them in `B`; being outside both constituents requires the defender's choices to span alternatives rather than stay wholly in either constituent. Thus the state is in `K`. The reverse inclusion is immediate, and induction on union-attractor rank proves that every later pure state is obtained by residual backward closure from `K`.

A direct corollary is **attractor subsumption ⇒ zero bridge**. If one constituent attractor contains the other, the union attractor equals the larger constituent.

This is not merely fitted to chess. An independent implementation tested **100,000 arbitrary finite turn-based graphs** with **0 two-target factorization mismatches**.

### 2.3 Hyperkernel Factorization — n targets

For target family `{Ti}`, let `A_J` be the attractor to any proper target subunion and let `B` be the union of all proper-subunion attractors. The irreducible n-way region is the full-union attractor minus `B`. A defender **hyperkernel** state lies outside `B`, has all replies in `B`, but its replies are not contained in any maximal proper-subunion attractor.

The same minimum-rank argument proves that the irreducible n-way region is exactly the residual backward closure of this hyperkernel. G6 machine-certified the three-way case over the b–g N/B/R atlas and the independent generic regression found **0 three-target mismatches** across 100,000 arbitrary graphs.

The chess consequence is genuine compositional depth: on d/e there are **2,739 knight** and **6,172 bishop** states requiring all three BK-left/same/right alternatives, while rook has zero.

`lift = slab coverage among mixed residual geometries − slab coverage among all residual geometries`.

Critical manifolds therefore form one **compression regime**, not a universal law.
