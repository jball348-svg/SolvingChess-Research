# 1. Starting evidence from G6
- G6 has a universal game-graph core: strict attractors, defender sanctuaries, Branch-Kernel and finite-n Hyperkernel composition.
- It has a strong resource-safe TARGET schema, broad conditional restoration theorems, and explicit terminal/rank semantics.
- It has at least four boundary regimes: selective timing manifolds, saturated clocks, transferable tactical grammars, and lookup-scale local floors.
- Proof graph composition can amplify small defender kernels into large certified regions (e.g. 4,302 → 117,430 in bishop+c-pawn).
- Terminal-model mistakes can change hundreds of thousands of outcomes, so G7 must type material-reduction and promotion continuations from the outset.
The implication is that G7 no longer needs to prove the existence of symbolic structure in another nearby four-/five-man ending. It should test whether the architecture remains useful when positions contain more simultaneous resources, more capture endpoints, more checking branches and multiple lower-material escape routes.
# 2. North-star questions
1.  Can a position-discovery process find tractable six- and seven-man finite windows without selecting them because they resemble G6’s successful topologies?
2.  When a higher-tree position has several legal exchanges/captures, can exact lower-material proof objects compose into meaningful coverage before local search begins?
3.  Do Branch/Hyperkernel mechanisms scale from alternative semantic targets to alternative material-transition endpoints?
4.  Can the resource-safe TARGET constructor identify useful stepping stones when both sides possess multiple mobile pieces and checking resources?
5.  Do defender sanctuaries remain useful higher up the tree, or are they destroyed by extra attacking resources?
6.  Which G6 representation regime predicts the new boundary: selective manifold, saturated clocks, transferable tactical grammar, or local floor?
7.  Can G7 discover a genuinely new proof-object type that is not merely a renamed G6 target/restoration rule?
8.  Do new higher-tree objects fire backward across the G6 corpus and either subsume old lemmas, sharpen their domains, or produce clean counterexamples?
9.  At what material/branching depth does proof-graph certificate cost begin to approach direct exact enumeration?

# 6. “Fire it back” protocol
G7 should treat transfer as bidirectional. A rule discovered higher up the tree is not “more general” merely because it solves a larger material class. It must be checked against the existing architecture and historical corpus.
1.  UPWARD FIRE: after a new position family is frozen, apply every type-compatible G6 proof object unchanged before any new symbolic search.
2.  COMPOSITION FIRE: represent lower-material exits as proof-graph nodes and test branch/hyperkernel composition across alternative capture/exchange endpoints, not only square predicates.
3.  BACKWARD FIRE: apply each promoted G7 object to G6 benchmark families and quantify exact subsumption, marginal coverage, conflicts and counterexamples.
4.  REFACTOR: if a G7 object strictly subsumes several G6 lemmas, replace them only in the new proof-language version while preserving frozen historical IDs and dependency provenance.
5.  NEGATIVE PRESERVATION: if a higher-tree rule fails on a G6 family, retain the counterexample and narrow the type/domain rather than patching square IDs.
# 7. Automatic discovery under proof discipline
- Candidate discovery may use decision trees, symbolic regression, SAT/SMT templates, small program synthesis or graph-feature mining.
- Features must be versioned semantic atoms: move/check access, material-transition labels, defended capture squares, promotion/fortress resources, relative geometry and exact lower-material object membership.
- No learned model is proof. Every promoted rule is flattened and exhaustively scanned on its stated finite domain.
- Hold out entire material/topology families, not random states from the same arena.
- Penalize absolute square identifiers and one-off constants unless they arise from symmetry-relative coordinate systems.
- Use description cost and certificate size as co-equal metrics with coverage.

These are hypotheses for discovery, not assumptions. If they reduce to existing G6 object types, that is a useful result; if they fail, preserve the falsification.

# 10. Advance criteria
- At least six prospective higher-tree families receive exact certified passes, with at least three material classes not present in G6 and at least two six-man-or-higher families containing multiple mobile pieces on opposing sides.
- Candidate selection is frozen before W/D/L inspection and is demonstrably driven by structural novelty/tractability rather than resemblance to G6 successes.
- At least two families contain three or more distinct exact material-reduction endpoints reachable by legal play.
- At least three G6 object types add material exact coverage unchanged in higher-tree families, or their failure domains are sharply characterized.
- At least one composition operates over alternative material-transition endpoints, not merely alternative square predicates.
- At least one new G7 abstraction succeeds prospectively on a held-out material/topology family and survives a backward-fire audit against the G6 corpus.
- A proof DAG of depth at least three typed operations certifies a region materially larger than any constituent base object.
- At least one higher-tree family yields a defender sanctuary/fortress result or a clean certificate that extra material destroys the lower sanctuary.
- At least two adversarial high-branch controls are preserved honestly, including complexity-floor outcomes if needed.
- Description/certificate cost is tracked against exact state growth so G7 can say whether ascent is economically plausible.
# 11. Failure criteria
- Most selected arenas are merely G6 pawn-file/rank variants with one decorative extra piece.
- Tractability restrictions are changed after outcome inspection to obtain attractive symbolic results.
- Lower-material dependency graphs become so underspecified or model-sensitive that exact terminal semantics cannot be frozen.
- G6 proof objects contribute negligible coverage across all higher-tree families and no reusable replacement emerges.
- New rules depend on absolute-square catalogues or material-class-specific constants and fail backward-fire tests.
- Branch/hyperkernel composition over material endpoints adds negligible value beyond direct exact solving.
- Proof description/certificate cost grows approximately with raw state count before reusable layers appear.
- Held-out performance collapses while within-family training compression remains high.
- High mobility/checking prevents any meaningful finite-window certification within the declared budget; if so, freeze the scale boundary rather than shrinking after truth inspection.
# 12. Stopping rules
- Stop a local grammar at the same lookup-scale discipline used in G5/G6 unless a parameterized family clearly transfers.
- Stop a candidate family if the next exact window exceeds certificate budget without changing the material-transition question.
- Stop architecture retuning after two consecutive higher-tree families show the same negative mechanism with no reduction in semantic exception cost.
- Do not stop G7 because one higher-material topology is hostile; G7 is a portfolio and negative controls are deliverables.
- Do not spend G7 repairing the two frozen G6 historical exceptions unless new implementation artifacts appear; they are provenance exceptions, not G7 scientific objectives.
# 13. Ambitious end-state
A successful G7 should leave the programme visibly higher in the chess tree. It should contain a frozen atlas of materially richer exact windows, an explicit transition DAG into G6-certified lower-material nodes, a measured account of which G6 proof objects survive ascent unchanged, at least one prospectively transferred higher-tree abstraction, and a bidirectional proof-language update showing what the new objects explain in the old corpus.
The strongest positive result would be a demonstration that a position with several mobile pieces and several exchange choices can be certified compositionally by funneling play into alternative exact lower-material objects, with branch/hyperkernel-style defender choice and a proof DAG whose description grows much slower than the exact higher-material state set. The equally valuable negative would be a precise branching/material threshold beyond which this architecture loses compression before a reusable layer emerges.
